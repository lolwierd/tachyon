Mock 01

Reading room

The reader is the subject. Continue reading dominates; the rest recedes to marginalia.

Cover at 128 × 192 — small enough to anchor, not dominate. Title absorbs the author as an italic subordinate clause, so the whole identity is one line. Tags are an italic serif run, not pills. Continue-reading carries the chapter fraction inline on the right side of the button (Ch 18 · p 1) so the reader sees exactly where they'll land. Auto-download is a thin strip between hero and chapters, framed by two hairline rules. It reads as a caption, not a card.

Mock 02

Catalogue card

The series as a single archival entry. Labels on the left, values on the right. A cinnabar seal presses the status into the top corner.

Mono labels (TITLE, BY, PUBLISHED, UPDATED, SYNOPSIS, TAGS, STANDING ORDER) anchor values to a left column that's six characters wide. Corner marks on the outer card are a quiet nod to a real archival envelope; you'll notice them if you're reading closely, not if you're not. The seal (READING · CH 17) is rotated 3 degrees so it looks stamped, not placed. The title gets right-margin to leave room for the seal. Actions sit outside the card as a due-date stamp would.

Mock 03

Broadside

Magazine-jacket composition. Title commands the optical top; auto-download migrates INTO the chapters shelf — where it belongs.

The cover is dropped below the title's baseline so the serif title lands shoulder-to-shoulder with the cover's top third, the way a book jacket headline sits against cover art. Tags are prose, not pills ("Tagged action, adventure, comedy…"). Auto-download has been reconsidered: it isn't part of the series' identity, it's part of chapter management. So it moves — into the Chapters section header, treated as a tool on a shelf next to Filter / Jump / Sort. This is the brief that 7 out of 10 designers wouldn't take. It's the right one.

How to choose

Mock 01 is the safest ship — small cover, big continue, auto-download as a caption strip. Works well if you value the library-home-style visual rhythm.

Mock 02 is the most conceptually committed — the whole top is one archival card with a stamped seal. Rewards slow looking; every row is a reading.

Mock 03 makes the radical claim: auto-download isn't a series-identity concern, it's a chapter-management tool. It belongs ON the chapters shelf, not above it. If you buy the claim, this is the cleanest outcome — the hero is purely about identity + continue, and the chapters section gets a proper tool shelf that will scale when future standing orders (cache policy, notification rule) land.
